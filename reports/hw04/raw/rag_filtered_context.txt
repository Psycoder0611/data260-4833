Randomization and Control Groups

Randomization assigns participants to study groups using a chance-based method. Its purpose is to reduce selection bias and make treatment groups more comparable.

A control group provides a comparison for the experimental treatment. A control may receive a placebo, standard treatment, or another intervention.

Randomized controlled trials combine random assignment with a comparison group to improve the reliability of conclusions about treatment effects.

Blinding in Clinical Research

Blinding reduces the chance that expectations influence study results.

In a single-blind study, participants generally do not know which treatment group they are assigned to.

In a double-blind study, both participants and relevant study personnel are unaware of treatment assignments.

Blinding can reduce measurement and expectation bias when it is practical and appropriate for the study design.

Clinical Trial Phases

Phase I clinical trials primarily evaluate safety, dosage, and side effects. They usually involve a relatively small number of participants.

Phase II trials evaluate whether a treatment shows evidence of effectiveness while continuing to monitor safety.

Phase III trials compare the treatment with existing treatments or standard care in larger groups of participants.
